| arm | prec | recall | F1 | **bal-acc** | **MCC** | acc [95% CI] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| grounded | 1.000 | 0.333 | 0.500 | **0.667** | **+0.370** | 0.545 [0.35, 0.73] |
| semantic | 1.000 | 0.467 | 0.636 | **0.733** | **+0.467** | 0.636 [0.43, 0.80] |
| fused | 1.000 | 0.533 | 0.696 | **0.767** | **+0.516** | 0.682 [0.47, 0.84] |

Three things follow, in descending order of importance.

## 1. The verifier catches gross category error and misses fine-grained identity error

This was not anticipated by the design, and it matters more than the arms comparison.

**Caught (8/15)** — the whole object is wrong:
`amur_leopard` (tiger-like head) · `anas_platyrhynchos` · `axolotl` · `durian` ·
`love_in_a_mist` · `monkey_puzzle_tree` · `zalophus_californianus` ·
`red_ferrari` (McLaren body)

**Missed (7/15)** — a recognisable member of the right category, wrong in its details:
`african_grey_parrot` (no white eye patch) · `boston_bull` · `bonsai_tree` ·
`cardinal_bird` (leg colour) · `hedgehog` (sparse spines) ·
`golden_retriever` (eyebrows, fur) · `polar_bear` (dog-like face)

The split is clean, and it is the wrong way round for this project. **Reference-guided
regeneration exists to repair fine-grained identity** — an Amur leopard that is merely a leopard, a
Boston bull that is merely a dog. The verifier passes exactly those cases. It fires reliably only
when the draft is grossly wrong, which is the easy case and the one a generic pipeline would already
handle.

Both streams share the blind spot, so it is not fixable by reweighting fusion. Stream A crops the
detected region and scores it against the phrase; a passable parrot scores well on "African grey
parrot" whether or not it has the eye patch. Stream B is asked a yes/no question about the whole
image and answers at the same granularity.

## 2. C1 is not demonstrated at this sample size — as predicted

Fusion beats the single VLM judge in the right direction on every headline metric, but the margin is
**+0.033 balanced accuracy — one extra true positive out of 15** — and the Wilson intervals overlap
almost entirely (fused 0.682 [0.47, 0.84] vs semantic 0.636 [0.43, 0.80]).

The honest statement is **"not separated at n=22,"** not "fusion wins."

The streams disagree on only **4/22 cases (18%)**: 3 `SEMANTIC_ONLY`, 1 `GROUNDED_ONLY`. So the
entire measured value of decomposing the single judge, on this sample, is one case that Stream B
alone missed. The parent spec (§2) offers stream disagreement as the evidence that decomposition
buys something; here it buys one case, within noise.

**All three arms do beat both constants** (0.667–0.767 vs 0.500 balanced accuracy, MCC +0.37 to
+0.52 vs 0.00), so the verifier carries real information. It is not a degenerate always-FAIL
detector wearing a good F1. That is a genuine, if modest, positive.

## 3. Precision is 1.000 on every arm, and τ barely matters

**Zero false positives across all three arms.** Nothing the verifier flags is a good image. Combined
with recall 0.533, the verifier is conservative: when it speaks it is right, but it stays silent on
half the failures. For a pipeline that regenerates on failure, that is the safe direction — it will
never destroy a correct draft — but it caps how much of the problem the method can reach.

**τ\* = 0.02 is fitted to noise.** It sits at the extreme edge of the swept grid, on a peak two grid
cells wide (0.767 at τ = 0.02–0.03, 0.695 by τ = 0.04). The `fused` curve is dominated by the
τ-independent semantic arm, and the shipped default `tau: 0.25` yields grounded balanced accuracy
0.624 against the peak's 0.667. **Do not adopt 0.02.** τ is not the lever here.

The Stream A abstain rate is **0%** at every τ, so the grounded arm was live throughout and `fused`
is not secretly Stream B alone.

## What this means for Plan 3

1. **Do not build the repair loop on this verifier as it stands.** It will pass most of the cases the
   repair step exists to fix, so the main table would measure repair quality on a biased subsample of
   the easy failures.
2. **The fine-grained detection problem is the real work**, and it is Stream A's natural territory:
   score the crop against *contrastive* alternatives (`African grey parrot` vs `grey parrot`) rather
   than against its own label in isolation, which is what the bake-off's `finegrained_seg.py`
   ancestor already did at 0.917 accuracy.
3. **Expanding the sample is cheap and worth it**, but it will not fix finding 1 — a bigger sample
   measures the same blind spot more precisely.

## Reproducing

```bash
./scripts/run.sh score-a --labels outputs/screen_20260726_233320/labels.csv
./scripts/run.sh score-b --labels outputs/screen_20260726_233320/labels.csv
./scripts/run.sh c1      --run    outputs/screen_20260726_233320
```

Stream A ~4 min, Stream B ~13 min at nf4, report seconds. Both stages resume per case. `outputs/` is
gitignored, so `c1.md` lives with the run rather than in git.

## Caveats

- n=22, 7 negatives. One false positive moves specificity 14 points. Every interval is wide.
- τ\* is selected on the same cases it is scored on. No held-out split exists at this size.
- Stream B ran nf4-quantised, not bf16, because the shared card OOM'd at bf16. Quantisation may cost
  judge quality; it was not measured.
- The `verdict` (any-visible-defect) column is reported alongside in `c1.md` and shows the same
  ordering at an 82% base rate.
